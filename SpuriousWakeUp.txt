A spurious wakeup occurs in multithreading when a thread waiting for a specific condition to become true is awoken from its waiting state even though no other thread explicitly notified it and the condition it was waiting for has not been met.

"Imagine a group of workers in a break room waiting for a delivery truck to arrive. They have a rule: 'Stay asleep until the delivery guy knocks.'
In a perfect world, they only wake up when the delivery guy actually knocks.
A spurious wakeup is like someone in the break room waking up because of a loud noise outside—maybe a regular car backfired, or a door slammed—not because the delivery truck arrived. They woke up, but the condition they were waiting for hasn't actually happened yet.
The Core "Reason" (Simplified)
The main reason this happens is the way operating systems are built:
Efficiency over Certainty: It is simpler and faster for the operating system to sometimes wake up a sleeping thread due to general system noise (like a timer interrupt or a different internal signal) than to ensure every single wake-up signal is perfectly aligned with the exact condition your program is waiting for.
The OS doesn't know your logic: The operating system just manages sleeping and waking threads; it doesn't understand the specific condition your application logic is waiting for (e.g., "is the queue empty?").
The Interview-Ready Solution
The important takeaway for an interviewer is that you know how to handle this possibility:
"Because we can't trust the reason for waking up, we always treat a wake-up as a hint to go check the condition again. We don't assume the condition is met. That's why in multithreading, we always put our wait() calls inside a while loop, never an if statement, to re-verify the shared state."
